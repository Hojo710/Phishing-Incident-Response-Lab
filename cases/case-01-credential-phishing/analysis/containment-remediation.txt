CASE 01 - CREDENTIAL PHISHING
CONTAINMENT AND REMEDIATION RECOMMENDATIONS

Evidence Preservation:
Preserve the reported phishing email and relevant header information as investigation evidence before removing or quarantining the message.

Email Containment:
Quarantine or remove matching phishing messages from affected mailboxes using the organization's approved email-security procedures.

Indicator Blocking:
Block the identified malicious sender, sender domain, Reply-To domain, and credential-verification URL/domain using the organization's appropriate email, web, DNS, or other security controls.

Scope Investigation:
Search the organization's mail environment for additional recipients of the same or similar phishing message. If additional recipients are identified, determine whether any users clicked the link, submitted credentials, downloaded content, or otherwise interacted with the message.

Reporting User:
Notify the reporting employee that the message was assessed as a credential-phishing attempt. Reinforce established phishing-reporting procedures and advise the user to avoid interacting with similar suspicious messages.

Credential / Account Response:
No password reset, session revocation, or account containment is recommended based solely on the evidence currently available. The recipient reported no interaction with the phishing link, and no evidence of credential compromise was observed.

If evidence is later identified that credentials were submitted or the account was accessed without authorization, escalate the incident and initiate appropriate account-containment procedures.

Endpoint Response:
No endpoint isolation is recommended based solely on the current evidence because no malicious attachment, download, execution, or endpoint compromise was observed.

If subsequent investigation identifies evidence of malicious execution or endpoint compromise, escalate for endpoint containment and further investigation.

Security Awareness:
Consider broader phishing-awareness communication through the organization's established security-awareness or communications process.

Analyst Recommendation:
Contain the phishing infrastructure and message while avoiding unnecessary disruption to the reporting user's account or endpoint. Expand the investigation if additional recipients, user interaction, credential exposure, or other evidence of compromise is identified.
