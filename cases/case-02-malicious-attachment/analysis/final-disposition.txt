CASE 02 - MALICIOUS ATTACHMENT
FINAL DISPOSITION

Classification:
Malicious - Phishing / Malware Delivery Attempt

Severity:
Low

User Interaction:
Not Observed - Employee reported that the attachment was not opened.

Endpoint Compromise:
Not Observed

Credential Compromise:
Not Observed


SUMMARY

The investigated email was determined to be a malicious phishing attempt designed to persuade the recipient to open a disguised executable attachment.

The email contained multiple suspicious indicators, including a mismatch between the sender and Reply-To addresses, failed SPF and DMARC authentication, no DKIM signature available for validation, urgency-based social engineering, and an attachment using the deceptive double-extension filename:

Employee_Salary_Adjustments_2026.pdf.exe

The final .exe extension presented the attachment as a Windows executable rather than a PDF.


STATIC ANALYSIS

Safe static-analysis procedures were practiced using a harmless text training artifact rather than the simulated executable.

SHA-256 hashing demonstrated that modifying file contents changes the resulting hash and that a hash can be used to identify and compare exact file contents without executing the file.

File-signature analysis of the harmless training artifact identified the beginning hexadecimal bytes:

43 41 53 45

These bytes corresponded to "CASE" in ASCII and were consistent with the contents of the plain-text training artifact.

No PE executable signature was observed in the training artifact. The Windows PE signature "MZ" was discussed only as a comparison and was not observed in the analyzed file.

Strings analysis demonstrated how readable artifacts can provide investigative leads without proving that referenced behaviors occurred.


SIMULATED DYNAMIC ANALYSIS

During the simulated sandbox scenario, execution of
Employee_Salary_Adjustments_2026.pdf.exe resulted in the following observed behavior:

- powershell.exe was launched.
- Network communication with update-payroll.example was observed.
- C:\Users\Public\payroll-update.exe was created.
- payroll-update.exe was subsequently executed.

These behaviors were observed only in the simulated sandbox environment and were not observed on the employee endpoint.

FINAL ASSESSMENT

The correlated phishing indicators and simulated sandbox behavior support classifying the email and simulated attachment as malicious within the lab scenario.

The employee reported that the attachment was not opened. No available evidence establishes execution of the attachment, malicious process activity, credential compromise, or endpoint compromise on the employee's system.

Containment should therefore focus on quarantining the phishing message, searching the email environment for additional recipients or matching messages, and determining whether any other users interacted with the attachment.

Endpoint or account containment should be escalated if additional evidence identifies execution, compromise, or unauthorized account activity.


EVIDENCE LIMITATION

This case is a controlled cybersecurity training scenario. The executable attachment and sandbox behavior are simulated.

The static-analysis exercises were performed against a harmless text artifact. No real malware was created or executed during this lab.
