TIMELINE CORRELATION

08:02:
Successful authentication from New York using the employee's known company laptop. MFA was satisfied.

08:14:
The simulated phishing email was received.

After 08:14:
The employee reported clicking the verification link and submitting their work email address and password.

08:37:
A successful Microsoft 365 authentication occurred from Amsterdam using an unknown/new device. MFA was not satisfied.


ANALYST ASSESSMENT

Event 1 is consistent with the available baseline information for the employee because the authentication originated from the known company laptop and MFA was satisfied.

Event 2 is anomalous because it occurred shortly after the employee reported submitting credentials to the suspicious website and differs from the earlier activity in geographic location, device status, and MFA result.

The rapid geographic change between New York and Amsterdam is consistent with an impossible-travel indicator. Geographic information alone would not establish compromise because IP geolocation, VPNs, proxies, and other infrastructure can affect apparent location.

However, when Event 2 is correlated with the employee's reported credential submission, successful authentication, unknown/new device, and differing MFA result, the available evidence supports unauthorized account access and account compromise.


EVIDENCE BOUNDARY

The available evidence establishes successful anomalous authentication to the employee's account.

The current evidence does not yet establish what actions were performed after authentication.

No claim is currently made that email was accessed, files were downloaded, inbox rules were created, additional credentials were obtained, or persistence was established.

Additional account-activity evidence is required to determine the scope of post-authentication activity.
