CASE 03 - CONTAINMENT AND ESCALATION

SEVERITY:
High

IMMEDIATE CONTAINMENT ACTIONS

1. Reset the password for employee@company.example because the employee
   reported submitting credentials to the phishing site and subsequent
   evidence confirmed unauthorized account access.

2. Revoke active Microsoft 365 sessions and authentication tokens to
   terminate existing authenticated sessions associated with the
   compromised account.

3. Preserve the unauthorized inbox rule configuration as evidence, then
   disable or remove the rule.

4. Remove the employee account's unintended Conditional Access exclusion
   and verify that the organization's MFA requirement for external
   sign-ins is correctly applied.

5. Review Microsoft 365 authentication, mailbox, and OneDrive audit logs
   to identify additional unauthorized activity and determine the full
   scope of the incident.

6. Investigate activity associated with source IP 198.51.100.219 and
   search for additional accounts or resources accessed from the same
   source.

7. Review the affected financial files and related vendor/payment
   processes for potential follow-on fraud risk.

ESCALATION AND NOTIFICATION

1. SOC / Security Leadership
   Escalate the incident to the SOC manager, incident response team, or
   appropriate security leadership because the investigation confirmed
   unauthorized account access and data exfiltration.

2. Identity / Microsoft 365 Administration
   Coordinate account containment, session and token revocation, password
   reset, Conditional Access remediation, and verification of MFA coverage.

3. Finance / Accounts Payable
   Notify the appropriate financial data owners because Confidential and
   Restricted financial information was accessed and downloaded. Review
   vendor and payment activity for potential follow-on fraud.

4. Legal / Privacy / Compliance
   Escalate the confirmed data exposure so the appropriate teams can
   determine whether contractual, regulatory, privacy, or notification
   requirements apply.

5. Management / Executive Leadership
   Notify appropriate management through the organization's established
   incident-response escalation process because of the High severity,
   confirmed account compromise, and financial-data exfiltration.

FOLLOW-ON BUSINESS RISK

The unauthorized user searched the mailbox for "invoice" and downloaded
vendor and payment information, including bank-routing information.

This creates a potential follow-on risk of financial fraud or Business
Email Compromise (BEC), including attempts to impersonate trusted vendors
or manipulate payment instructions.

No follow-on BEC or payment fraud has been observed in the available
evidence. This is a potential business risk identified from the confirmed
incident activity.
