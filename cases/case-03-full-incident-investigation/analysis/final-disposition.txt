CASE 03 - FINAL INCIDENT DISPOSITION

FINAL CLASSIFICATION:
Malicious - Credential Phishing Resulting in Cloud Account Compromise
and Confirmed Data Exfiltration

SEVERITY:
High

INCIDENT SUMMARY:

The employee received a simulated Microsoft 365 phishing email containing
a link to a credential-harvesting website. Although SPF, DKIM, and DMARC
passed for the sender domain, the domain was a lookalike and the email
used urgency and account-restriction language to encourage interaction.

The employee reported clicking the phishing link and submitting their
work email address and password to the simulated login page.

Following the credential submission, a successful unauthorized Microsoft
365 authentication was observed from source IP 198.51.100.219 using a
new/unknown device.

Review of authentication controls determined that the employee account
had been unintentionally excluded from the organization's Conditional
Access policy for external sign-ins. No MFA challenge was issued during
the unauthorized authentication. The available evidence therefore does
not establish an MFA bypass.

POST-COMPROMISE ACTIVITY:

Activity associated with the unauthorized session included:

- Mailbox access
- Creation of an unauthorized inbox rule
- Mailbox search for "invoice"
- OneDrive access
- Successful download of three financial files

The inbox rule was configured to identify security-related messages,
mark them as read, and move them to Deleted Items. This functionality
was consistent with an attempt to conceal unauthorized account activity.

DATA IMPACT:

Three files were successfully downloaded during the unauthorized session:

1. Q3-Financial-Forecast.xlsx - Confidential
2. Vendor-Payment-Details.xlsx - Restricted
3. Department-Budget-2027.xlsx - Confidential

The affected information included financial forecasts, department budget
information, vendor/payment information, invoice numbers, and bank-routing
information.

The successful unauthorized downloads establish confirmed data
exfiltration of these three files within the simulated incident.

EVIDENCE BOUNDARY:

The available evidence does not establish exfiltration of additional
files beyond the three identified files.

The investigated inbox rule contained no forwarding address and therefore
was not configured to forward email externally. This does not rule out
other possible methods of email access or exfiltration.

No follow-on Business Email Compromise or payment fraud was observed.
Such activity remains a potential follow-on business risk based on the
financial information exposed during the incident.
